Case Study 03 · Sorry! World · Hasbro IP Pre-Launch

Working inside a 1929 IP — discovering what it could become in a form it had never existed in before

Gameberry Labs' inaugural collaboration with Hasbro for the first standalone mobile app of the Sorry! board game (established 1929). The application modernized the classic board mechanic through character customization, town-building, league progression, and layered meta systems — while preserving the familiar pawn-and-board foundation.

01 — The Context

The principal challenge involved discovering creative possibilities within an already-established, globally protected IP identity — rather than defining one from scratch. The creative tone emphasized playful warmth and social, friendly rivalry.

The constraint wasn't the IP. The constraint was finding what the IP could become — in a form it had never existed in before.
02 — Team Configuration

When the project required additional creative depth, I suggested deploying a motion design lead and storyboard artist — the same configuration that had succeeded on MatchStar. When motion direction still needed advancement to meet the Creative Director's standards, I joined alongside Draw Match responsibilities. The final team included a Creative Director (vision holder), a creative trio (motion lead, storyboard artist, creative strategist), a Principal Motion Graphics Designer (traditional animation expertise), a Spine animator (pip character system), two mid-level designers, and one junior designer.

Taking on Sorry! World alongside Draw Match was possible because of what the team had become — not despite it.
03 — Creative Approach + Pipeline

Pip animation library: Spine-animated character system created reusable libraries across multiple styles, animation states, and expression levels — optimized through feedback and templatised for production consistency.

Modular video architecture: four independently-built gameplay videos served as self-contained pieces. A fifth trailer — directed by the principal MGD — was assembled from those modular elements rather than functioning as a standard highlight reel.

Quality benchmark video: the first gameplay video, developed collaboratively by the principal MGD and me, established the motion direction standard for all subsequent team work. Every aspect — pacing, character timing, FX behavior, board interaction rhythm — was deliberately resolved.

The pip characters — central visual elements — required solving first. The Spine animator constructed a comprehensive animation library across different character styles, emotional expression states, and energy variations. Each iteration received team feedback, and successful results became templatised systems rather than rebuilt components for each video. The principal MGD's traditional animation foundation — understanding weight, timing, and expression — established the quality ceiling. Characters needed specific qualities: cheeky tone, warmth, physical grounding despite stylization.

Enough exaggeration to make the pips feel alive and distinctly Sorry! — but controlled enough that the gameplay always remained easy to follow on first watch.
04 — Licensed IP Workflow

The Hasbro approval process required designing around rather than against. Rather than presenting speculative creative, the team developed meticulous understanding of the IP's visual language, tone, and emotional territory before submission.

When Hasbro reviewed the benchmark video, feedback was minimal — essentially a single logo detail — and largely approving. They recognized a Sorry! reimagination they hadn't conceived themselves, yet felt authentically aligned with the IP. This is the distinction between compliance-based licensed work and interpretation-based licensed work done effectively.

05 — Accumulated Capability

Sorry! World represented the third distinct creative challenge within a compressed timeframe — each fundamentally different in character:

Draw Match: building creative identity from absence, within high ambiguity, before product existence. MatchStar 3D: establishing structured experimentation at scale. Sorry! World: working within inherited creative identity while discovering unrealized potential under approval constraints and parallel project demands.

The project's successful execution evidenced accumulated team capability — strategic deployment of individuals to specific gaps, refined production thinking, and understanding of marketing requirements.

Not a single project — but a compounding body of capability — built through each challenge, available for the next one.
